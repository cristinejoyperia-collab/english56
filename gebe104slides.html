GEBE 104: Mathematics 6 Assessment & Reporting

Competencies & Learning Objectives

🎯

3 Target Competencies

  1. (1) Illustrate ratio and proportion using tables and/or the double number line model;
  2. (2) Find how many times one value is larger than another given their ratio, and vice versa;
  3. (3) Solve problems involving ratio and proportion.
📚

Derived Learning Objectives

  • Define ratio, proportion, and equivalent ratios;
  • Read and interpret ratio tables and double number lines;
  • Express ratios in simplest form;
  • Find missing values in proportions;
  • Classify direct, inverse, and partitive proportion; and
  • Solve multi-step real-life ratio problems.

4 Computation Steps & Worked Example

  1. (1) Percentage Score (PS): PS = raw score ÷ highest possible score × 100
  2. (2) Weighted Score (WS): WS = PS × weight
  3. (3) Initial Grade: Sum of the three WS values
  4. (4) Transmutation: DO 8 Table (Appendix B) maps 60.00 to the passing grade of 75.
Worked Example Using Class Data:
If a learner's QA percentage score were 51 (your class MPS), the transmuted grade calculation is:
60 + (0.4 × 51) = 80.4 → rounds to 80.

Ethical Issues in Assessment & Reporting

🔒

Confidentiality

Protect learners' personal information, grades, and assessment results. Share only with authorized guardians and never announce publicly.

⚖️

Fairness

Give all learners equal opportunities to demonstrate their knowledge and skills using objective, bias-free assessment methods.

🤝

Inclusivity

Consider learners' different abilities, backgrounds, and needs so assessment activities remain flexible and supportive.

🏷️

Avoiding Labeling

Avoid negative labels like "slow learner," "weak," or "poor performer." Focus on strengths, progress, and next steps.

Core Reminder

"As teachers, we assess learners to help them grow, not to judge, compare, or define their worth."

Evidence-Based Recommendations for Improving Practice

🎯
DIFFERENTIATED REMEDIATION

1. Implement Targeted Remediation & Differentiated Instruction

Use the TOS and item analysis to target least-mastered competencies (Items 9, 11, 13, 15, and 16). Use concrete objects, ratio tables, double number lines, real-life activities (recipes, transportation, shopping, sharing), the "I Do, We Do, You Do" approach, small-group support, enrichment tasks, and Peer-Assisted Learning (Fuchs et al., 2002; Thurston et al., 2020).

🔍
DATA-INFORMED FEEDBACK

2. Strengthen Item Analysis, Formative Assessment & Constructive Feedback

Examine both difficulty and discrimination indices (Paul et al., 2026) to refine problematic items and guide review on ratio interpretation (Item 11) and direct proportion (Item 15). Embed regular formative checks—such as a 3-item exit ticket during the last 5 minutes of class—and provide immediate, reasoning-focused feedback (De Sousa, 2007).

🤝
COLLABORATIVE REPORTING

3. Strengthen Transparent, Inclusive & Collaborative Reporting

Communicate specific mastered competencies and growth areas alongside numerical grades (DepEd Order No. 8, s. 2015). Encourage simple home activities (comparing prices, adjusting recipes, measuring ingredients), hold regular parent-teacher conferences, uphold confidentiality, and strictly avoid negative labels such as "slow learner" or "poor performer."

💡 Reflection and Conclusion

The analysis of the Grade 6 Mathematics assessment shows that assessment is not simply about administering exams and computing grades—it is a vital tool for identifying strengths, uncovering learning gaps, improving instruction, and monitoring growth.

With Grade 6–Pearl's 51% mean score and the least-mastered items identified through item analysis, combining differentiated instruction, peer-assisted learning, formative assessment, constructive feedback, and ethical parent-teacher collaboration ensures learner-centered, evidence-based support for every child.
